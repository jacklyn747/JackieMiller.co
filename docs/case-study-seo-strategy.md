# Case-study SEO strategy — "search the problem, not the profession"

*Captured 2026-09-04, mid-session, at Jackie's request ("remind me of this later").*

## The insight (Jackie's words, paraphrased faithfully)

**The case studies need to work standalone — as an SEO strategy.** That means choosing *very carefully* what each case study is **about**.

**Why it's important:** almost nobody knows what an "instructional designer" is. People don't search for "an instructional designer." **They search for the thing they need.** So the case studies have to be built around the searchable problem/outcome the audience is actually looking for — not around the job title or ID jargon.

## Implications to work through later (Claude's synthesis — not yet decided)

- **Each case study is a landing page for a real search.** Its title, H1, URL slug, meta description, and framing should map to what a prospective client or hiring manager would actually type — the *problem*, *skill*, or *outcome*, not "instructional design."
  - e.g. someone needs "computer skills training for people re-entering the workforce," or "onboarding that people actually finish," or "a course built without a real budget" — not "an instructional designer."
- **This may change what the case studies are titled and even which projects we feature.** "Digital Literacy Fundamentals" is closer to a course name than a search term; the searchable framing might be different.
- **Connects to the hero decision (open):** if each case study is a standalone SEO entry point, its hero should foreground the *searchable problem/outcome* for clarity and ranking. That suggests the **cinematic B&W portrait-of-Jackie hero belongs on the homepage / about page** (the personal-brand front door) — not on each project page, where the topic should lead. (To confirm with Jackie.)
- **Pairs with the tooling we have:** the `searchfit-seo` skills (keyword clustering, content brief, on-page SEO) can turn this into real target keywords per case study once the topics are chosen.
