## 1. The Blueprint

### Snapshot
- **Marcus, 50** *(pseudonym)*
- **27 years incarcerated** — locked up before smartphones, before the app economy, before crypto existed.
- **Texas state unit. Tablet only — no laptops here.**
- **Works HVAC on the unit** — regular hours *plus* call-outs at all hours; when a system goes down,
  he goes, day or night. His time isn't just tight, it's **unpredictable** — he can't count on a block
  of it.
- **Recently denied parole.**
- **Currently attempting:** an intro **cryptocurrency** course and a **personal finance** course.

### What he wants
To walk out ready. Not just paperwork for the board — actual skills for a world that reinvented
itself while he was gone. He signs up for courses genuinely wanting to learn. That's the part that
makes the rest hurt.

### The arc — why he starts a lot and finishes little
Marcus isn't unmotivated. He's *discouraged*, and those are different problems with different design
answers. And the discouragement rarely arrives as one big wall — it's **friction that stacks** until
putting the tablet down is the easiest thing in the room:

1. **Unpredictable time.** HVAC calls come at all hours on top of his regular shift. He'll get pulled
   mid-lesson, and when he comes back he's lost his place — or worse, has to start the video over.
2. **A body with nowhere to sit.** No desk. He's on the steel bunk or the toilet, tablet on his knees,
   back and neck aching before the video's half done.
3. **Noise he can't turn off.** The dorm is one room — TV up loud, dominoes slapping the table, guys
   hollering across the run. In a Texas summer with no AC, it's the heat on top of all of it.
4. **The parole denial underneath it all.** He finished courses before; the board didn't seem to weigh
   them either way. So when the friction stacks, the quiet question is already there: *if finishing
   changes nothing, why sit through this?*

No single one of these stops him. **Together**, every time, the path of least resistance wins — and
he's back in the dayroom instead of in the course. That's the real failure the design has to fight:
not one barrier, but the way small ones compound.

### His real context (the design almost never accounts for this)
- **No quiet.** Living space and dayroom are one room. He studies inside the noise — TV, dominoes,
  hollering, the fans — nothing he can lower.
- **No desk, no comfort.** He's on the bunk or the toilet, tablet on his lap, hunched over. Texas heat,
  no AC. There is nowhere designed to sit and think.
- **A tablet, not a laptop** — but some courses are built as if he has a laptop.
- **He can be pulled at any moment** — an HVAC call doesn't wait for him to finish the module.

### Friction map — what he hit → what it demands
| What Marcus experienced | The design demand it creates |
|---|---|
| Course assumes a laptop; he has a tablet | **Design for the device he actually holds** — tablet-first, touch-first |
| Pulled out by an HVAC call mid-lesson; loses his place | **Resume where he left off** — save state, no penalty for being interrupted |
| Fails a quiz → forced to rewatch the **entire** video, no pause/rewind | **Video as a resource, not a gate** — scrub, rewind, review one part |
| Has pen and paper, but no pause — can't stop to grasp a concept and write it | **Pausable playback that makes room for note-taking** — the video waits for the learner, not the reverse |
| Rewatching a long video hunched on a bunk in the heat and noise | **Respect the body and the room** — short segments, works in fragments, no forced sit-throughs |
| Quizzes are multiple-choice / summaries only — he can pass without understanding | **Assessment that teaches**, checks *comprehension*, not just recall |
| An abstract, unfamiliar concept (crypto) taught by passive video + a quiz | **Scaffolding to build a mental model** — analogy, visual/worked examples, interactive concept models — so a topic with no reference point becomes graspable |
| Finance course, no built-in calculator | **The right tool, in context** — an on-screen calculator where the math happens |
| Small frictions **stack** until quitting is easiest | **Lower the cost of continuing** at every step — the pile is the enemy, not any one piece |
| Effort felt disconnected from any payoff | **Visible progress + intrinsic competence** — the motivation the system can't supply |

### In his words *(pull-quotes, drawn from the interview)*
- *"I'd start them. I just wouldn't finish. After the board said no, it was like — what's the point? It didn't matter to them either way."*
- *"They want you on a laptop. We don't have laptops in here. Just the tablet."*
- *"You miss the quiz, you gotta watch the whole thing over. Fifteen minutes, sitting on the steel toilet 'cause there's no desk, back killing me, TV blasting, dominoes going — in the heat. And halfway through they call me out for a work order. Come back, gotta start it again. After a while you just put it down."*
- *"Crypto — I've been down 27 years. I've never touched any of it. Reading about it isn't the same as doing it, and there's no doing it."*

## 2. The simulated session *(the animated self-typing chat)*

**Label on the page:** *A simulated research session — Marcus is an AI-simulated composite, grounded
in real interviews with Edovo users. This exchange is scripted from what real learners told us.*

**Framing:** Jackie (designer) in a research conversation with the persona. Keep it tight; each beat
exposes one friction point and builds to the heart.

> **Jackie:** You signed up for the crypto course a while back. How far did you get?
>
> **Marcus:** Started it. Didn't finish it. That's kind of my pattern, if I'm honest.
>
> **Jackie:** What stops you?
>
> **Marcus:** It's never just one thing. I work HVAC on the unit — they call me out at all hours, not
> just my shift. So I'll be in a lesson and get pulled for a work order. Come back, I've lost my spot.
>
> **Jackie:** And picking it back up?
>
> **Marcus:** That's the part. No desk in here — I'm on the bunk or the toilet, tablet on my knees,
> back aching. TV's up loud, dominoes slapping the table, guys hollering, and it's Texas hot, no AC.
> Now I gotta sit through a fifteen-minute video *again* in all that. Any one of those, fine. All of
> it at once? You just set the tablet down and go do something else.
>
> **Jackie:** Why start it over — can't you go back to where you were?
>
> **Marcus:** Nah. Miss the quiz, you watch the whole thing from the top. And there's no controls — no
> pause, no going back to the part you missed. I've got my pen and paper right there, but it won't stop
> for me. Soon as a concept lands and I go to write it down, the video's already three steps ahead.
> Fifteen minutes to get back to the one thing you got wrong.
>
> **Jackie:** And the material itself — crypto?
>
> **Marcus:** That's a whole other thing. I've been down 27 years — that world got built while I was
> gone, so I'm starting from nothing. And it's not like a trade you can put your hands on; it's all
> abstract. Somebody talking at me on a video, then a quiz. I can memorize enough to pass the quiz, but
> I couldn't sit here and tell you I actually understand it. There's nothing helping me *get* it.
>
> **Jackie:** If you could change one thing about how these are built —
>
> **Marcus:** Let me *do* something. Give me a calculator in the finance class. Let me stop the video
> and write it down. And let me pick back up where I left off — I'm gonna get called out, that's the
> job. Treat me like I'm trying to learn it, not just trying to pass it.

## 3. The refinement it drove

Marcus didn't just illustrate problems — he changed specific decisions. *(Tie each to the real
case-study fix during the rebuild.)*

- **Built for interruption** → progress saves automatically; he resumes exactly where he was pulled
  away, with no penalty. The job can call him out and the course waits.
- **Content broken into short segments** → learnable in the fragments of time and comfort he actually
  gets, instead of one forced fifteen-minute sit-through.
- **Video became a resource, not a gate** → pause / rewind / scrub so he can stop the moment a concept
  lands, write it down, and pick the video back up; failing a check returns you to the relevant moment,
  not the top.
- **Scaffolding for abstract, unfamiliar concepts** → for something intangible like crypto, analogy,
  visual worked examples, and interactive models that build a mental picture — plus checks for real
  understanding, not recall — so a topic with no reference point becomes graspable.
- **The right tool, in context** → an on-screen calculator inside the finance material.
- **Tablet-first, touch-first layout** → designed for the device he actually holds.
- **Progress made visible** → the system supplies the sense of forward motion the parole process
  never did.

**The through-line:** the blocker was never one wall — it was *friction stacking* on top of a quiet
*"does this even matter?"* Good learning design can't fix a parole board. But it *can* refuse to add
to the pile: lower the cost of continuing at every step, respect the body and the room and the
interruption, and give competence you can feel — so the effort counts moment to moment, even when the
system upstream won't say it does.
