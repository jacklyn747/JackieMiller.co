## What he said (faithful capture)

### Completion — starts a lot, finishes little
- Started **many courses, finished few**.
- Two reasons:
  1. **Time constraints** — he has a prison job, so course time competes with work.
  2. **The bigger one — a psychological block from a negative parole answer.** After being denied parole, he felt that finishing courses *wouldn't matter* — the parole board doesn't seem to care one way or the other whether he completes them. That discouragement became a block: *why finish, if it changes nothing?*

### Hardware mismatch
- Some courses **assume the learner has a laptop.** That may fit one unit, but where he is in Texas **there are no laptops — only tablets.** The course design doesn't match the device he actually has.

### Physical environment
- **Noise / shared space.** You're doing coursework around other prisoners — dominoes, activity, noise. Some facilities have a separate living space and a separate day room; **his is all one room**, so there's no quiet place to focus.
- **No desk.** Some prisoners have to sit **on the toilet or on the top bunk** and do everything **on their lap** — physically uncomfortable, no writing surface.

### Interaction / pedagogy problems
- **Fail a quiz → rewatch the ENTIRE video.** There's no pause, no rewind, no scrubbing. You can't stop to take notes or go back to review one part. Failing means starting the whole video over.
- **Evaluation is shallow / boring.** Quizzes are **only multiple choice**, some are summaries. Nothing more engaging or varied.
- **No practical, hands-on component.** This makes it much harder to learn something you have **no prior familiarity** with. Example: he's taking a **cryptocurrency** course — completely new to him after 27 years inside — with **no way to get any hands-on experience** with it.
- **Missing tools that would obviously help.** A **finance course** would be far more useful with an **on-screen calculator** built in.

## First-pass themes (for the persona blueprint)

1. **Motivation is extrinsic-gated.** The single biggest completion blocker isn't ability or interest — it's a belief that the effort is unrewarded (parole indifference). Design implication: intrinsic payoff and visible progress/competence must carry the motivation the system can't.
2. **Design assumes the wrong context.** Assumes laptop, desk, quiet, ability to control playback — he has tablet, lap, noise, and locked linear video. Every assumption that doesn't hold is a friction point.
3. **Playback is punitive, not a learning aid.** Locked linear video + full-rewatch-on-fail treats the video as a gate, not a resource. No note-taking support, no review.
4. **Assessment doesn't teach.** Multiple-choice-only + summaries = recall, not application. No hands-on, no simulation, no tools-in-context (calculator, crypto sandbox).
5. **Learning cold-start.** Brand-new domains (crypto) with zero real-world anchor and no practice are the hardest — exactly where hands-on matters most, and exactly where it's absent.
